The Caravan Out
At Kolt's Depot you signed on with Kolt for a caravan escort south to Ironhold. You named yourselves The Wayward Company — three of you, one wagon of your own driven by Orin, hauling Sylas's two undead labourers and a fresh draft horse from O'Leary's that Orin christened Valor. Matilda was still missing, her tracks still leading north. Brenna Hartwell, the caravan master, walked you through the manifest — twelve crates of mixed cargo split across her lead wagon and a contract wagon — and one of those crates was a sealed iron-bound chest bearing an eclipse seal, sun half-eaten by shadow. Brenna would not say who it was for; she said only that sealed crates paid extra and that she did not ask what was inside.

Torren looked at the seal and knew it. Coin Cipher. The network's mark. He said only enough for Sylas and Orin to understand what that meant — do not interfere, the client is rich, nobody asks what's inside. Sylas cast Detect Magic on the chest later that evening and picked up a necromantic aura, which confirmed nothing anyone wanted to discuss aloud.

You rode out on Day 1 with Brenna's lead wagon at the front, the contract wagon in the middle, and your own Wayward Company wagon in the tail. The daytime was uneventful in the way that made you uneasy — a scorched circle in a field, a Watch rider heading north with news, the sense that the road was quieter than it should be. You made camp in a stone-ring the road obviously uses for that purpose. Sylas cast Detect Magic again on watch and picked up a soft healing-and-enchantment aura from Brenna's kit — quiet confirmation she came prepared, with a Potion of Superior Healing and a Smoke Brick in her belongings. Orin's watch was reflective — Merric, Seraphine, and the light. Torren's watch was quiet apart from an owl in a nearby tree that watched him back. Late that night Sylas and Torren spoke privately about the chest. Torren wanted to open it; Sylas argued for the long game — let it be delivered, use Locate Object to track it after. You agreed to wait.

Day 2 brought Stillbrook. You stopped briefly at the village well and Orin looked at the brook, which was visibly wrong — dark, sluggish, oily on the surface. He concluded it wasn't spoiled, that something was contaminating it upstream, and that whatever it was, it was acidic and natural rather than spilled or magical. It was at the well that Sera Vey approached you — wounded, no coin, desperate. Her sister Mira and Mira's two children had been taken three days ago by a man called Halvor of Ironhold over a farming debt. Sera had tried to intervene when they were taken and been knocked down. She needed to reach Ironhold to find Mira. Torren rolled Insight with Expertise and hit a natural 20 — a 35. She was honest, in over her head, and had nothing else. You accepted her. She rode hidden in the Wayward Company wagon with Orin and the zombies and did not sleep well. An hour further down the road you passed a dead cow with acid burns on its hide. Sylas confirmed the burns came from a creature, not a chemical, and the threads began to connect.

Then a shadow crossed the road. Then a second time. Vrass — a young black dragon, sleek black scales still slick with old marsh water — landed in front of the caravan and demanded you abandon the wagons and run. There was no way through this by force. Torren dismounted, walked forward, and knelt. He praised the dragon, asked its name, offered the caravan. Sylas produced a healing potion as tribute. Vrass wanted more — he wanted a servant. And Torren agreed. A lifetime of servitude — his own — to Vrass, in exchange for the party's lives and safe passage. Persuasion natural 20. Torren did not try to be clever with the wording. Vrass accepted, called Torren "mine," and took the contract wagon, lifting it away toward his lair somewhere upstream of Stillbrook. While the negotiation dragged, Sylas quietly moved the sealed Coin Cipher chest out of the wagon and into his Bag of Holding. Vrass did not see it happen. Brenna did not see it happen. Vrass took the crates and flew off believing he had everything, but he did not have the chest. His parting words were, "We will speak soon."

Losses: the contract wagon and half the caravan cargo — foodstuffs, cloth, finished metal goods. Retained: Brenna's lead wagon, your Wayward Company wagon (Orin, Sera, the zombies), and the Coin Cipher chest now hidden in Sylas's Bag of Holding. Brenna was shaken but respected what she had seen. She could not force the merchants to pay for what Vrass took, but the twenty-five gold pieces she personally promised for the escort still stood, and she would tell the merchants what happened when you reached Ironhold. You pushed on through the afternoon and Ironhold's smelting fires appeared on the horizon at last light — a dull orange glow visible from a full day out. You camped just outside the town's northern gates and ate mostly in silence.

Late that night, Sylas and Torren spoke privately again. The chest was safe in the Bag of Holding. What comes next was harder to answer — whether Ironhold has someone willing to fund an expedition against a dragon, whether Torren's servant role becomes a way to get inside Vrass's operation, whether there's a way through this that does not end with Torren's life belonging to a black dragon forever. Brenna had a name in mind for a possible funder in Ironhold, though she wouldn't say more until you arrived. Nothing was settled. It's all still open.

Long rest. Level up to 6. You're camped outside Ironhold's northern gates on the evening of Day 11 — 24 Frostfall — awaiting dawn and the gate opening. The Wayward Company is Torren, Sylas, and Orin, level 6, camped at Ironhold's northern gate. Travelling with you are Brenna (parting ways at the gate), Sera (finding her sister at dawn), two zombies, and Valor. You are carrying the sealed Coin Cipher chest, hidden in Sylas's Bag of Holding. And on Torren, a lifetime oath sworn to a young black dragon called Vrass — what that means in practice, you do not yet know.

Sessions 3–4
The Gnoll Den and What Lay Beneath
Hired by Oskar, the Goliath Townmaster of Aeloria, you were sent to deal with gnolls threatening the southeastern farmlands. You tracked them to their den and put an end to the pack.

But what you found inside changed everything.

A shrine — not to a gnoll deity, but to Naturus, the Betrayer God of rot and decay. The pack leader had been charmed and marked by an outside hand. And writings on the walls spoke of a larger plan: rot spreading through the Aelorian territories, guided by unseen hands. One note pointed to a location you already knew well: The Pig's Head Inn.

Back in Aeloria, the party split. Torren returned to O'Leary's Stables to check on Matilda — only to find her gone, spooked and escaped. Only tracks remained, heading north.

At the Pig's Head, Orin pressed patrons for information about a name: Merric. A missing friend. A past not yet buried. Through connections to a figure called Rulden, Torren gained access to a private meeting — and the truth turned out to be deeply personal. Rulden is Merric.

The next morning, a message arrived for Orin. Cold. Careful. Signed only "R." It ended:

"Keep to the light, Orin. It suits you better."

Orin sought guidance at the Temple of Luminos. Dawnwarden Seraphine listened — and gave her counsel: if Merric once walked in the light, and if you feel called to him still, then you should not stop.

The party now stands at Kolt's Depot. A caravan to Ironhold awaits. But behind you, threads remain unresolved — and a hidden network continues to spread rot through the land.

The Shard and the Letters
You were brought together by Vaeloran Duskwhisper, the archmage of Aeloria — a respected scholar-mage known for his charitable works and civic dedication. He set you two tasks: recover his missing apprentice, Lyssandra Vale, and deliver a set of sealed letters to members of Aeloria's ruling council on his behalf.

You rode north out of Aeloria on the trail of the meteor — about a day's travel into the wooded territories where the rock had struck. The clearing felt wrong: strange energies in the air, wildlife behaving oddly. You fought through corruption-warped spiders to reach a lonely barn nearby, where Lyssandra had been hiding.

She was afraid. She tried to burn the barn and flee rather than be brought back. Before disappearing into the night, she warned you:

"He'll twist your good intentions… like he did mine."
"That shard… it's not just a rock. He's been studying fragments like it for years."

You tracked her north through the wilds for five days, catching her on the road before she could reach Stonegate. She was brought back to Vaeloran, who thanked you warmly and said the shard had corrupted her mind — and that he would see to her recovery. The letters were delivered separately in the chaos.

1st Age — Creation and Harmony

The Creator Gods shaped Eldoria together — bringing light, order, and the celestial realms, and infusing the world with life, forests, rivers, and the balance of the natural world.

They gave rise to the Children — ten lesser gods, each holding a domain. For a long age, the Pantheon lived in harmony.

But two of the Children grew restless. One coveted the power of secrets; the other hungered to spread decay. Slowly, through whispers and cunning, they turned others against the Creators.

The Usurpation — End of the 1st Age

The Betrayer Gods rose against the Creators. The battle scarred the world. The Creators were driven into hiding; their power was diminished but not destroyed.

In the shadow of that war, the Dwarven clans of the Underpeak were shattered by pressure from the Aurum Dominion. Those who refused submission began the great exodus — the ancestors of what would become the Celestium Clans beneath the Celestial Plateau.

2nd Age — The Dark Reign

The Betrayers ruled openly. The sun's light was dimmed and a long twilight settled across Eldoria. Forests twisted into thorn-mazes; rivers ran foul; predator and prey lost their balance.

Fear spread. Dark cults flourished. Loyalty was demanded and dissent punished.

In the marshes to the west, the Lethraliri civilisation endured — a people of ice and water, builders of stilt-cities and communers with elemental spirits. Their role in the Age's end became legend.

In hidden places, followers of the Creators whispered to one another through dreams. Heroes rose beneath the banner of the returning light.

The Reclamation — End of the 2nd Age

The Creators returned. With the Children who had remained loyal, and with mortal heroes who had never given up hope, they cast the Betrayers down. The Betrayers were bound — the priests say — beyond the reach of mortals, and the sun rose over Eldoria for the first time in seven centuries.

Much was lost. Much was rebuilt. The current age began.

3rd Age — Reclamation and Reforging (present day)

The age you live in. Civilisations have rebuilt. The Aelorian Territories, the Aurum Dominion, the Celestium Clans, the Sylvarian Enclaves, the Ember Wastes nomads, the Reptilian Tribes and the Pirate Confederacy all hold their own patches of the world.

Trade routes have reopened. Temples of the Creators have been raised again in most cities — though not all: in the Aurum Dominion, the faith of the light is quietly suppressed by the state.

But the corruption the Betrayers left in the world was never fully cleansed. In the deep places, in the marshes, in the wild forests — something still stirs.
